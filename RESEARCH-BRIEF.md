## Three candidate framings

### A · The measurement problem *(methods-first)*

> Galaxies reach us as light. Turning light into mass, age and history is the hard part —
> and doing it properly keeps overturning what we thought we knew.

Then: Prospector → known unknowns and burstiness → ML and SBI → the JWST surprises that
fell out of it.

**For:** it is the genuine through-line of your whole career, from the factor-of-two
problem to today. Very few people own "galaxy SED inference" the way you do, so it is
defensible territory. It also explains *why* your JWST results carry weight.
**Against:** a methods framing can read as drier than "we found impossible galaxies" to
a journalist or a prospective student.

### B · The early universe broke our expectations *(discovery-first)*

> JWST found things that should not exist. I lead the modelling that decides whether
> they are real.

Then: universe-breakers → little red dots and black-hole stars → what it takes to
believe a measurement → the tools that make it possible.

**For:** maximum resonance, and it matches your actual media footprint — NPR, the
Guardian, the Atlantic, CNN. Puts the newest and hottest work first.
**Against:** risks reading as a JWST bandwagon rather than as the person with the durable
methodological contribution. "Surprises" age; in five years this paragraph is stale.

### C · Three pillars *(recommended)*

A short lead paragraph carrying A's intellectual through-line, then three co-equal
pillars rather than a narrative arc:

1. **Little red dots and the early universe** — LRDs, black-hole-star atmospheres,
   RUBIES, UNCOVER
2. **How galaxies actually form stars** — burstiness, star formation histories,
   population models, outshining
3. **The machinery** — Prospector, `cue`, neural emulators, simulation-based inference

Plus a two-line closer on what's next (PFS, the wide-survey era).

**For:** scannable, and each pillar is independently updateable — which matters for a
site that went five years without an edit. It maps one-to-one onto
`content/research/*.md`, so adding a pillar is adding a file. It also gives each group
member's work a natural home.
**Against:** less of a story, more of a structured catalog.

**Why I'd pick C:** the failure mode of this site has been going stale, and C is the only
option that stays true when the news moves. The lead paragraph still gets to carry the
argument.
